[research] fin

31 July 2026

I survived my final presentation! it ended up being 60+ slides, the majority being plots, plots, and more plots. bubble plots, networking cluster plots, Venn diagrams, tables... and all that jazz. I felt so bad for my lab members having to sit through me going over each plot. I was bored listening to myself talk, which is always a bad sign. I'd mentioned to Raul before about my concern with there being too many plots in the presentation, but he said it was necessary to include everything. then after my presentation, Giorgio said there were too many plots and they did not all need to be in there. hah. oh well, it's done now.

Giorgio also questioned the whole methodology behind the analysis, and the fundamental value of doing an exploratory analysis (like the one I did) on the given dataset. while it hurt to hear in the moment, this is valuable feedback. Philipp showed interest in the results and seemed to think with some more work, this analysis could be turned into a small paper. I'm not sure if he's just being nice, but I will err on the side of caution and assume he is. I did meet with Giorgio and Raul after my presentation, and we discussed potentially continuing to collaborate on this project (with some reframing beforehand) after I return to the US. I'd like that -- I'd like to turn this work into something useful and maintain my connections with the people here.

overall, this summer of research taught me a lot of R and bulk RNA-seq analysis methodologies. it also exposed me to some different (and perhaps less conventional) paths into research, and different ways that a career in research could look day-to-day.

the people here come from a range of different backgrounds and career stages, so it's cool that this research centre brings them all together, at least for the here and now. and I'm grateful to be part of it, at least for the here and now.
