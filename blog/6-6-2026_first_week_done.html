[blog] first week done

6 June 2026

that's a wrap on week 1 in Newcastle. beyond my first day in the country, I really haven't felt homesick at all, which is honestly surprising, knowing myself. perhaps it's because the people I've met, both through work and within my accommodations, have been so warm and welcoming. I do anticipate some homesickness to creep in starting from week 6 onward, based on my past experience studying abroad in Singapore.

my first day in the office, I was handed over to Raul, who would be supervising my summer project. at first glance, he seemed a normal fellow -- if anything, a bit stiff, since he was dressed formally in a collared button-up, slacks, and brown Oxfords. I soon realized this couldn't be further from the truth. that first day, he invited me to eat lunch with him and some others at the centre, and they all seemed interested in meeting the newcomer from the States. after lunch, he parked himself next to my desk to chat about summer project logistics, and the conversation ended up meandering into a broader discussion on academia, career prospects, and life. he's definitely a yapper, but I'm grateful that he seems willing to sit down with me and answer my questions. it makes him very approachable, someone who is open to (maybe even welcomes) being "bothered".

after work that first day, I wandered into an Indian restaurant on my route home called My Delhi for some curry and naan. it was incredible. I came to the UK prepared for bland, beige food, but I'm quickly realizing that the international influences around this city have blessed its people with a variety of cuisines spread throughout the streets.

on Tuesday, I got invited to eat lunch with Asmita (another woman in my lab) and her lunch buddies, which was an honor for me. getting to chat with so many different people about their work and lives, and perceiving the effort people are going to to make me feel welcome here, is very gratifying. Asmita then asked me if I wanted to get dinner later that week, so we went out for Uyghur food and croffles on Friday! I think I'm just surprised how sociable and friendly people are here in the workplace compared to my UF lab.

I'm a sucker for outdoor markets, so I made my way to Armstrong Bridge this morning (Saturday) for the biweekly Jesmond Food Market. it was a drizzly one, and pretty small -- just one or two rows of stalls lining the the length of the bridge. I procured a Georgie banger (sausage in a bun) that was too much bun for my taste, as well as a Scotch egg and two savory pies for later. slowly exploring the pillars of British food culture! I was also debating going for fish and chips from Hake Foods (they had a food truck at the market), but I wasn't hungry enough. it looked really good though, so maybe next time.

I then explored a bit of Jesmond Dene, a big public green space connected to several parks, which Armstrong Bridge actually crosses over. the arching canopy of branches overhead and the sounds of birdsong being blown through the leaves by the wind created a lovely oasis of nature, with steep banks leading down to a river (and a waterfall further upstream).

that's one thing I'm loving so far about Newcastle -- how much well-maintained public green space can be found throughout the city, complete with locals enjoying the nature with their dogs, as well as birds, mice, and even cows (on the Town Moor)!
