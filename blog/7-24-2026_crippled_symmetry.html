[blog] Crippled Symmetry

24 July 2026

one of the things on my bucket list when coming here was to attend a music performance at the Glasshouse (in Gateshead, just across the River Tyne from Newcastle). from the outside, it looks like a giant amorphous blob of glass divided into thousands of window panes. in other words, quirky and grand.

I finally committed earlier this week and bought tickets (with Malika, Raul, and Raul's partner Andrea) to a 90-minute performance of 20th-century composer Morton Feldman's "Crippled Symmetry", taking place today after work. my first impression of the venue (Sage Two) was of stepping into the bottom of a beehive -- if I were the size of a bee. there were two rows of seats arranged in rising levels in a circle around an open space containing the instruments (one piano, one celesta, one silver saxophone thing, and a bunch of xylophone-looking contraptions). then there was a circular balcony one floor up that looked down on the whole scene, and a high domed ceiling escaping into shadow, away from the dim lighting.

that's where the fun part ended. I sat with Raul on my right and Andrea on his right (Malika had purchased her ticket separately and was seated across the room), anticipating the music. but when the three artists started playing, there was no discernible melody! the music seemed very abstract, discordant, random -- basically just not like music. but I guess that depends on how you define music, which is probably a subjective thing.

anyhow, within 10 minutes I'd given up waiting for the music to pick up and resigned myself to 80 more minutes of struggling to stay awake through this weird waking musical dream of wandering through a strange landscape. probably what saved me was watching the people around the room also struggle to stay awake. at least half of the people I observed nodded off at one point or another. some of the balcony viewers above had their heads resting on their arms or hands, eyes shut. I think my favorite, though, was a college-aged woman in a fancy red dress who'd come with (I'm assuming) her partner. at one point, her head was flopping into the next row back, with her hair reaching toward the feet of the people behind her and her mouth hanging open.

I really wanted to leave during the performance but didn't want to be rude. I'm impressed with myself for staying awake through the whole thing. the venue was definitely the best part of the experience.

after the performance ended, our party of four walked down to the river for some drinks at an outdoor seating area in the fading light of day. we laughed about our different individual experiences with the music: Andrea had absolutely hated it; Malika didn't get it at first, but she'd started recognizing patterns toward the end; Raul had enjoyed a 90-minute nihilist noir daydream backed by "Crippled Symmetry". it had been obvious as we were walking out that most of the other attendees also couldn't wait to escape.

do I regret going to this? I'm not sure. it was a humorous experience for sure. I have mad respect for the musicians -- I can't imagine this kind of music is pleasurable to practice, and each full run-through would take 90 minutes (at least). if I hadn't gone to this, the Wombats (a UK indie rock band I've never heard of before) happened to be holding a concert this evening in the plaza outside the research centre. their sound check this afternoon sounded promising -- I could've snuck back into the office and listened from up there... or stood outside the boundaries of the concert and rocked on for free.
